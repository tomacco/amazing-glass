Web components for HTML, React and Vue. They match Apple's Liquid Glass to about 2 values out of 255 per pixel. One more line gives them a look of your own.

One material. Your light.

02

Blur is not glass.

Most "glass" on the web is a blurred rectangle. Real glass has thickness. Light meets the curved rim, bends, and carries the background with it. Watch the stripes at the edges.

03

Light bends at the edge.

For every pixel we take the slope of the rim, bend the view ray with Snell's law, and store how far it moved. Chromium runs that map as a filter on whatever is behind the element. Three passes with slightly different strength, one per colour, give the thin rainbow at the rim. Take one apart.

09

Two lines to start.

One CSS file, one script. Every element works in any framework. React and Vue get typed wrappers with the usual props, events and v-model. A look is one attribute more.

Copy

Where it works. Full refraction runs in Chrome, Edge, Arc, Brave and other Chromium browsers. Safari and Firefox do not run SVG filters inside backdrop-filter, so there you get frost, tint and rim light, plus real refraction over canvases the page painted itself. The hero runs on the experimental WebGL2 renderer, which should work everywhere WebGL2 does. Every feature is labelled stable, limited or experimental in the guide.

Where the looks come from. Each look is a homage to a movement: Light and Space, the Glass Chain, the stained glass of Saint-Denis and the Bauhaus. None is affiliated with an artist or an estate. Looks are experimental: the API and the colours may change.

Whose idea. Liquid Glass is Apple's design language, and their Human Interface Guidelines are the reference for every control here. This project is independent and not affiliated with Apple. Icons are our own drawings.
